DOC-01 // HOW THE LOOP IS MODELLED

How it works

CONTROLLER

The controller is the parallel (independent) form: u = Kp·e + Ki·∫e dt + Kd·de/dt.

The derivative term acts on the measurement rather than the error, so a setpoint step does not produce a derivative spike. The output is clamped to 0–100%, and integration is frozen while the output is saturated — that is what stops the integral term from winding up.

PROCESS

The plant is a first-order plus dead time (FOPDT) model: a time constant of 6 seconds, a steady-state gain of 1, and 1.2 seconds of transport delay between the controller output and its effect on the measurement.

Dead time is the reason tuning is not trivial: the controller keeps acting on stale information, which is exactly what makes aggressive gains oscillate on real loops too.

OVERSHOOT

How far the process variable travels past the setpoint, as a percentage of the step.

SETTLING TIME

The last moment the response was outside a ±2% band around the setpoint — after that it stays inside.

RISE TIME

Time taken to travel from 10% to 90% of the setpoint — a measure of how quick the loop is.

STEADY-STATE ERROR

The remaining offset at the end of the run. Proportional-only control always leaves some.

DOC-02 // THINGS TO TRY
  • P only — the response is fast but settles below the setpoint and stays there.
  • Add I — the offset disappears, and with enough Ki the loop starts to oscillate because of the dead time.
  • Add D — a little derivative damps the oscillation and cuts the overshoot.
  • Over-tuned — too much of all three: watch the output saturate at 100% and the response ring.
